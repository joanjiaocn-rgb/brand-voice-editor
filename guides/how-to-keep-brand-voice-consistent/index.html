Guides / Voice consistency

Daily practice

How do you keep brand voice consistent?

Keep brand voice consistent by giving everyone the same small set of rules, examples, and review questions, then building those decisions into the drafting workflow. Consistency comes from repeatable habits, not from one person rewriting everything.

Create one source of truth

Keep the approved voice statement, traits, word choices, examples, and tone guidance in one easy-to-find document. Do not split the rules across a brand presentation, an old content brief, and comments in three writing tools.

The document should be short enough to use during work. Link to deeper examples when necessary, but keep the decisions that affect every draft on the first page.

Add voice checks to the workflow

  1. Brief: name the reader, desired action, and appropriate tone.
  2. Draft: write for meaning and accuracy before polishing the voice.
  3. Edit: apply the voice rules to openings, structure, vocabulary, and rhythm.
  4. Review: check facts, claims, commitments, and situational tone.
  5. Learn: save strong new examples and record recurring exceptions.

This sequence prevents voice editing from hiding factual problems. A draft can sound perfectly on-brand and still be wrong.

Keep the voice stable across channels

Channels change the format, not the identity. A LinkedIn post may use a stronger opening, an email may make the request explicit, and a help article may explain each step in order. The underlying vocabulary, directness, point of view, and respect for the reader should remain recognizable.

HomepageHigh compression and clear positioning.Core vocabulary and point of view.
EmailRelationship, request, and context.Directness and respect for time.
LinkedInOpening, pacing, and scanability.Evidence standards and perspective.
SupportMore detail, empathy, and instruction.Plain language and honest limits.

How should you review AI-assisted drafts?

Treat AI output as a draft from a fast external writer. Give it the same voice rules, but review more carefully for invented details, generic claims, flattened opinions, and phrases your brand would never use. Never assume that a fluent sentence preserves the original meaning.

  • Compare names, numbers, dates, links, and commitments with the source.
  • Remove unsupported claims or experiences.
  • Check whether the first paragraph reflects the real point.
  • Replace generic enthusiasm with specific evidence.
  • Read the draft aloud to catch rhythm that does not sound natural.

Review patterns, not personal taste

Once a month, sample work from different channels and writers. Look for repeated drift: overly long openings, a return of banned jargon, inconsistent claims, or tone that misses the situation. Update training and examples around the pattern instead of correcting individual preferences sentence by sentence.
