Guides / Build your voice

Practical framework

How do you build a brand voice?

Build a brand voice by studying your best existing writing, defining the relationship you want with readers, choosing a few contrasting voice traits, and testing those choices on real messages. The result should guide decisions, not merely describe a personality.

1. Collect writing that already feels right

Start with evidence. Gather five to ten pieces of writing that sound closest to the business at its best: emails customers replied to, homepage copy that still feels accurate, support answers people understood, or posts the founder would happily publish again.

Mark recurring choices. Look for how the writing opens, how quickly it reaches the point, which words appear often, how long sentences run, and what has been deliberately left out. A strong voice usually already exists in fragments.

2. Define the reader relationship

Voice becomes clearer when you specify the relationship between brand and reader. "Friendly" is vague. "A capable specialist explaining the trade-offs to a busy peer" gives a writer much more direction.

  • What does the reader already know?
  • What kind of authority has the brand earned?
  • Should the brand lead, advise, challenge, reassure, or simplify?
  • What would sound patronizing or unnecessarily distant?

3. Choose three useful voice traits

Select traits as ranges with boundaries. This prevents a positive quality from being pushed until it becomes a weakness.

We areWhich meansBut not
DirectLead with the decision or next step.Blunt or dismissive.
WarmAcknowledge the person and the situation.Overfamiliar or sentimental.
ExpertUse precise language and explain trade-offs.Dense, academic, or full of jargon.

4. Turn traits into writing rules

For each trait, record two or three decisions that appear on the page. For a direct voice, that might mean putting the request in the first paragraph, replacing abstract nouns with verbs, and cutting ceremonial openings. Add a short "avoid" list for habits such as inflated claims, forced enthusiasm, or rhetorical questions.

A voice trait is useful only when two writers can use it to make the same editing decision.

5. Test the voice across real situations

Rewrite a homepage headline, sales email, support apology, product update, and short social post. If the voice works only in marketing copy, it is a campaign style rather than a durable brand voice. Revise any rule that breaks when the context becomes more sensitive or practical.

Keep the first version small. One page of rules and examples will be used more often than a long presentation. Expand it only when a recurring question appears.
